Resolving Behavioral Issues

Understand the behavior. Address the cause. Build lasting progress.

Unwanted behaviors are rarely as simple as they appear. Barking, chewing, reactivity, and other behavioral challenges can stem from fear, frustration, excitement, stress, unmet needs, learned habits, or underlying medical concerns.

At ClockWork Canine, we believe effective behavior modification begins with understanding why a behavior is happening.

Our Resolving Behavioral Issues courses are designed to help you recognize potential causes, understand your dog's body language, identify contributing factors, and develop an appropriate plan for addressing the behavior. Rather than treating every dog the same way, each course begins by exploring the reasons behind the problem before introducing strategies to help you work toward improvement.

What You'll Learn

Reactivity & Overstimulation
Learn to recognize the differences between fear-based reactivity, frustration, excitement, and other possible causes of reactive behavior. Explore triggers, body language, threshold management, and ways to build calm behaviour around people, dogs, and unfamiliar situations.

Biting, Nipping & Mouthing
Understand why dogs bite or nip, whether related to puppy teething and development, a history of improper play, fear, frustration, discomfort, or defensive behavior. Learn how to identify contributing factors, manage situations safely, and encourage appropriate alternatives.

Excessive Barking & Whining
Investigate what your dog may be communicating through vocalization. Explore possible causes including alerting, attention-seeking, frustration, fear, boredom, discomfort, and separation-related distress.

Separation Anxiety & Distress
Understand the difference between separation-related distress and other difficulties with being alone. Learn about gradual independence-building, appropriate management, and structured approaches to helping dogs who struggle when separated from their people.

Digging & Destructive Chewing
Explore the reasons dogs dig, chew furniture, or destroy household items, including instinct, exploration, boredom, stress, and unmet behavioral, physical, or mental needs. Learn how to manage the environment and encourage appropriate outlets for these behaviors.

Urine Marking & Humping Behaviors
Understand why dogs engage in urine marking, mounting, and humping, including the potential roles of hormones, excitement, stress, and learned habits. Learn to distinguish marking from incomplete housetraining and identify situations that may trigger mounting behavior. Explore appropriate management strategies, consistent training, and ways to address contributing factors, while understanding when veterinary guidance may be helpful.

Jumping Up & Attention-Seeking Behaviors
Learn why dogs jump on people, paw for attention, or repeatedly demand interaction. Develop consistent ways to teach alternative greetings and train more appropriate ways to ask for attention.

Resource Guarding
Recognize behaviors associated with guarding food, toys, resting places, or other valued resources. Learn about potential contributing factors, safe management of the issue, and how to fix it.

Fearfulness & Environmental Anxiety
Help identify situations that make your dog uncomfortable, from unfamiliar people and noises to new environments and everyday handling. Learn how to build confidence in your dog and slowly re-introduce the world to them in a new light.

Our Approach: Diagnose Before You Train

Every course follows the same core principle:

  1. Understand the behavior. Examine when it occurs, what precedes it, what follows it, and what your dog may be experiencing.
  2. Identify possible causes. Consider body language, environmental triggers, learning history, daily routines, and other contributing factors. Recognize when a veterinary or qualified behavioral assessment is needed.
  3. Build an individualized plan. Choose appropriate management and training strategies based on the likely cause, your dog's needs, and the safety of everyone involved.
  4. Practice, evaluate, and adapt. Monitor progress, adjust the approach when necessary, and recognize when additional professional support is appropriate.

Every Behavior Has a Context

Our goal is to help you understand your dog, address the factors contributing to the problem, and develop safer, more desirable behavioral responses.

Some challenges can be improved through consistent training and environmental changes. Others, particularly severe anxiety, aggression, and persistent distress, may require veterinary care or individualized assistance from a qualified in-person dog professional.

ClockWork Canine helps you take a more informed approach, giving you the knowledge to recognize the problem, understand your options, and make better decisions for your dog.

Don't just ask how to stop a behavior. Start by understanding why it happens.
